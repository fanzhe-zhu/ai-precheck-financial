Method DESIGNED FOR CONTAMINATION-FREE MEASUREMENT

System under test: a frontier model acting as a pre-submission checker under a structured prompt (five check categories; two-layer number verification — transcription and definition/basis; flags with location, source citation, confidence). No application code — the prompt is the v0.1 implementation, and it is the riskiest assumption worth testing first.

Corpora: Two realistic ~2-page earnings notes built from real filings (NVDA Q1 FY27; AMD Q1 FY26 as a holdout), each with 20 blind-injected errors across transcription, definition/basis, note↔model, rating language, prohibited wording, and disclosures — with a difficulty gradient including 6 deliberately deep-buried errors — plus 33–35 verified-correct anchors per corpus to measure false positives.

Protocol: Corpus construction (with answer key) and checker runs live in separate sessions so the checker never sees its own exam being written. Scoring: strict (any unsupported flag counts against precision) and lenient (corpus-flaw and low-confidence flags excluded). Runs: v1 ×5, v2 ×6, scored by hand against the answer key.

Findings EACH ONE IS A DESIGN INPUT, NOT JUST A SCORE

1 · EVIDENCE-DRIVEN CHECKING IS SATURATED OUT OF THE BOX
Transcription, definition/basis, cross-document ties, totals, footnotes, segment splits: 20/20 with near-zero prompt engineering and zero variance across runs. The moat is therefore not "can the model catch it" — any frontier model can. It is workflow integration, triage presentation, and the policy rule library. The eval independently re-derived the product's strategic shape.
2 · POLICY KNOWLEDGE MUST BE ENCODED, NOT ASSUMED
The one stable v1 miss (H17: a rating term off the house whitelist) was a policy-knowledge failure, not a reasoning failure — 0/3 across runs. Adding ~150 words of explicit house rules fixed it to 3/3 with zero regression (120/120). Implication: the rule library is a first-class product asset, and per-desk rules (learned from each desk's own bounce history) are the compounding part.
3 · A SIDE EFFECT BECAME THE SIXTH CHECK
The v2 triage rule systematically promoted unsupported factual assertions (consensus claims, product attributions with no source) to formal flags — technically "false positives" against the answer key, but arguably correct pre-submission behavior. Adopted as a sixth check category; next corpus will plant unverifiable-claim errors deliberately.

Limitations WHAT THIS EVAL DOES NOT SHOW

  • Synthetic ground truth. Planted errors, not real SA bounces. The real distribution of content-vs-taste rejections lives in publishing systems — obtaining it is the point of the proposed shadow-mode phase, not something this eval can substitute for.
  • Retrieval untested. Correct source pages were hand-fed. Finding the reconciliation table inside a 200-page 10-Q is a separate, unevaluated problem.
  • Corpora now saturated. Both sets stopped discriminating at v2 and are demoted to regression suites; the next capability step needs a third corpus (new issuer, unverifiable-claim subtypes, multi-quarter trend claims, table-vs-prose contradictions).
  • Rationale hallucinations. Verdicts were reliable, but supporting explanations occasionally contained small errors that flipped between runs — motivating a UI that visually separates verdict from reasoning, with the SA reviewing the latter.
  • Latency and cost at scale untested (excerpted corpora, not full filings). Hand-scoring is the current bottleneck; scoring rules are now stable enough to convert to an LLM-as-judge with human review of boundary cases (~70% cost reduction next cycle).
